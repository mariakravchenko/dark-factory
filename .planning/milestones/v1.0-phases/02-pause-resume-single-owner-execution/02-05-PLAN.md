<objective>
Expansion slice two: prove the heartbeat half of the lease, and prove the invariant that makes it necessary. A genuinely slow step keeps its lease and is never falsely stolen (SC5); no database transaction is ever open while the worker call runs, which is why a slow step needs a heartbeat rather than a held lock (HITL-08); and a lease that *is* lost mid-step aborts the step with nothing persisted, fenced at the commit rather than only by an in-memory flag.

This is where the roadmap's Open Decision 2 stops being a recommendation and becomes a validated property. The decision was recorded in code in plan `02-02` — a lease TTL of 900 seconds and a heartbeat interval of 300 seconds, both `Settings` fields — with the explicit note that the ratio, not the absolute value, is what transfers. These tests inject a two-second TTL and a one-second interval and prove the ratio behaves as claimed, in seconds rather than minutes.

Purpose: satisfy HITL-08 and success criterion SC5, and complete the HITL-07 story with the mid-step-loss case.
Output: three proof files, and any hardening of the lease and runner that the proofs turn up.
</objective>

  <behavior>
    - A transition applied with an owner token that does not match the run's current owner token raises the lease-loss error and commits nothing: the run's status, its latest role-execution row and its event count are all unchanged.
    - The lease-loss error is distinguishable from the concurrent-transition error, so a caller can tell "someone took my lease" from "someone moved the run past the status I expected".
    - A step whose lease is stolen while the worker call is in flight — simulated by overwriting the run's owner token from another session mid-step — persists nothing at all: no role-execution update, no status change, no event.
    - After such an abort the run is exactly at its last committed state, so the next process re-runs the step on the same attempt and the outcome is indistinguishable from the step never having started.
    - The in-memory lease-loss flag causes the abort to happen before any write is attempted, and the token fence causes the abort even when the flag is somehow not set, so removing either one still leaves the run protected.
    - The process that aborts exits non-zero, because an aborted step is not a clean stop and the operator should see it.
  </behavior>
